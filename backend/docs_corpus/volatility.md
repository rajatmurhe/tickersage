Volatility measures how much an asset's price fluctuates over time. It is commonly calculated as the standard deviation of returns over a period. Higher volatility means larger and more frequent price swings in both directions.

Volatility is often annualized so that different periods can be compared: the per-period standard deviation is multiplied by the square root of the number of periods in a year. Crypto assets typically show much higher volatility than major stock indices.

Volatility describes the size of past moves. It does not indicate direction, and past volatility does not guarantee future volatility.
