---
title: Funding rate

A funding rate is a periodic payment exchanged between traders who hold long and short positions in a perpetual futures contract. Perpetual futures have no expiry date, so exchanges use funding to keep the contract price close to the spot price of the underlying asset.

When the perpetual contract trades above the spot price, the funding rate is usually positive and long position holders pay short position holders. When it trades below spot, the rate is usually negative and shorts pay longs. The payment is made directly between traders; the exchange does not keep it.

Many exchanges settle funding every 8 hours, although some contracts use shorter intervals. The rate is quoted as a percentage of position size per funding interval, so a rate of 0.01% on a 10,000 USDT position means a payment of 1 USDT for that interval.

A persistently high positive funding rate is often read as a sign that leveraged traders are crowded on the long side, and a negative rate as crowding on the short side. It describes positioning, not where price will go next.
